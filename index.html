Source references stay attached to every calculation.

Every number has a source. So does every spread.

A quantity can say how uncertain it is: ~ ±5%, ~ 0.72..0.775, ~ 95% 67500..73000 for a published interval, or a named shape such as normal(12%). Each spread ends with @source or assumed, and the checker refuses one without. Drag an underlined number, including the ends of a range, and everything recomputes. A value dragged outside its own declared range is an error until the range is widened: the spread no longer describes it.

The answer is a distribution.

Monte Carlo propagation runs the whole program 100,000 times in a background worker, each time drawing every uncertain input from its declared distribution. The spread of answers is the result’s distribution: 90% of runs fall between P5 and P95. While you drag, a 4,096-run preview uses the same random numbers, so the shape moves instead of flickering. Inputs are drawn independently; correlations are not modelled. A distribution is only as good as the spreads declared, and assumed ones are labelled.

Which uncertainty matters.

The tornado nudges one input at a time by a fixed percentage. The variance share asks a different question: how much of the result’s spread comes from each input’s actual uncertainty. The solid bar is the first-order Sobol index, the share that knowing that input exactly would remove. The hatched extension is its total index minus the first-order one: the share it causes through interactions with other inputs. Estimated with Saltelli’s sampling design from (k + 2) × 16,384 runs, with 95% bootstrap intervals.
